# Start here

We built this repository for your interview. Have a look before we talk. There's nothing to
submit and you don't need to install anything.

## What it is

Permit Desk is the service behind a building permit portal for the City of Mountport. There's
no such city. Someone applies for a building permit, and this is the thing that walks the
application through zoning, plan review, a round of corrections, fees, payment, and issuing
the permit at the end. Four other city systems make the actual decisions. Permit Desk
coordinates them and keeps the record of what happened.

It's a working system rather than a snippet, and it's written the way a real municipal team
under deadline would have written it. We haven't annotated anything or tidied it up for
display.

## What we'd like you to do

Read it. An hour or two is plenty. Skim if you'd rather skim.

The README covers the architecture and how to run it if you want to. Running it is optional
and we won't ask whether you did.

Then turn up with opinions. Stuff we'll want to hear about:

- what you noticed
- what you'd fix first, and why that one and not something else
- anything you'd want to know before this went live to the public
- anything you'd leave alone

That last one isn't filler. Knowing what not to touch is most of the job.

## Using AI

Point an AI at it if that's how you work. Most people will, and we'd rather see how you
actually operate than watch you pretend otherwise. Nobody is going to ask whether you did.

It'll hand you a list. What it won't tell you is which two or three things you'd stop a
launch over, which ones you'd live with, or what you'd have the team do on Monday. That's
the part we care about and it's most of what we'll talk about.

## The interview

An hour, with two of us. Roughly half of it is this codebase.

We'll start by asking what you'd stop a launch for. Then we'll pull up a few places in the
code and ask what you make of them. After that it's yours to steer, go wherever you think
is interesting. We'll finish on what the first week of work actually looks like.

You won't be asked to write code and there's no take-home.

## Worth saying up front

We're not counting how many problems you find. Six things and a clear argument about which
one matters most beats twenty in a list.

"I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" is a fine answer. So is "I'd go ask whoever owns
that service." That's the actual job most days.

There's nothing you have to spot in order to do well. It's a codebase, we're going to talk
about it, and mostly we want to see how you think.

## If something's unclear

Ask us before we talk. It won't count against you.
